The founding insight

Two patients with identical first-order values can diverge dramatically. The unexplained remainder is not noise. It is interaction.

VₐF names the pathological class where structure is silent and function kills. Prinzmetal was its first member. Spasmodic dysphonia, we will argue, is its second.

Case study · Step 1

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has adductor spasmodic dysphonia — a focal laryngeal dystonia. The methodology demands we commit to the compartments before invoking Ω.

The diagnosis

Adductor spasmodic dysphonia produces a strained, strangled voice with abrupt phonatory breaks during speech — but not during laughing, whispering, or coughing. The muscles are histologically normal. The nerve is normal. There is no denervation, no myopathy, no structural lesion.

This is the phenomenological signature that matters: normal anatomy, abnormal deployment, task-specific spasm, stress-sensitive, reversible.

The key muscles — β₁S

For adductor SD, the literature points overwhelmingly to the intrinsic laryngeal adductors:

Overactive: thyroarytenoid · lateral cricoarytenoid · interarytenoid
Secondary: cricothyroid · suprahyoid · infrahyoid ("strap") muscles

These are unambiguously skeletal muscle. They are anatomically β₁S. But the spasms are not voluntary — which is precisely where the axiom's partition reveals its depth.

The methodological rule

Parsimony protocol

Do not invoke Ω until every first-order β term is exhausted. Speech already decomposes naturally into S, Vᵢ, VₐS, VₐF. The task is not to explain Ω — it is to ask whether the phenomenon submits to the compartments, and if so, which ones carry non-zero weight.

The criterion

β₄VₐF membership criterion
  • Intact effector — normal histology, no structural lesion
  • Episodic misdeployment — spasm without structural cause
  • State/stress sensitivity — modulated by autonomic or emotional state
  • Task specificity — preserved in some contexts, broken in others
  • Reversibility — recovers between episodes
  • No denervation, no myopathy, no fibrosis

Why the label stays

VₐF is not renamed "functional spasm disorder" or any other abstraction. The label holds its etymology — vascular functional — as an acknowledgment of the founding case. Entropy wasn't renamed when it escaped thermodynamics into information theory. The label points to the origin; the interpretation expands.

The enriched interpretation — one paragraph

VₐF denotes dynamic gain-control failure of an intact effector. The vascular case is the canonical instance. The criterion is phenomenological, not anatomical: normal histology, reversible spasm, state dependence, no structural lesion. Any effector system meeting this criterion belongs in β₄.

Treating VₐF as anatomical rather than phenomenological — missing the founding clinical experience that generated the category.
